Aim Training Drills: Daily Practice to Sharpen Your Aim

PUBG: Battlegrounds Guide • Updated November 2026 • 7 min read

Every PUBG player has lost a fight they should have won because their aim let them down. The enemy was in the open, the crosshair was close, and yet the shots went wide. Aim feels like talent, but it is a trainable skill, and the sharpest players are usually the ones who practice deliberately, not the ones who play the most matches. Twenty minutes of focused drills a day beats a hundred casual matches, because drills isolate the exact skills that matter and repeat them until they become automatic.

Set Up Before You Train

Before you start any drill, lock in your settings. Changing your sensitivity every week destroys the muscle memory you are trying to build, so pick a sensitivity you are comfortable with and leave it alone for at least a month. A good starting point on PC is a general sensitivity around 40 to 45, with an aim-down-sights sensitivity of around 35 to 40 for red dot and holographic sights. Use the same sensitivity in training mode and in matches, and use the same weapons you use in real games: the M416 for spray practice and the Kar98K or Mini 14 for flick shots.

Warm up with a few minutes of slow, deliberate mouse movements, then set a clear goal for the session: today flick shots, tomorrow recoil control. Random practice helps a little, but focused practice on one skill at a time helps a lot.

The Four Core Drills

Good PUBG aim is made of four skills: flicking to a target, tracking a moving target, controlling recoil during a spray, and switching between targets. Train all four across the week rather than grinding only the one you enjoy most.

1. Flick shots

Flicking is snapping your crosshair onto a target quickly. In training mode, stand at medium range from the target dummies, pick a target, flick to it and fire one shot, then flick to the next. Start slowly and focus on accuracy, then build speed. Practice with the Kar98K and the Mini 14, since these are the weapons where a fast, accurate first shot matters most. Ten minutes of flick practice a day will noticeably sharpen your first-shot accuracy within two weeks.

2. Tracking

Tracking is keeping your crosshair on a moving target. In training mode, use the moving targets or strafe alongside a dummy while keeping your crosshair glued to it. Fire in short bursts, and focus on smooth, continuous movement rather than jerky corrections. Tracking matters most against enemies who strafe, and it separates players who land three hits from players who land ten.

Pro Tip: Train at the ranges you actually fight at. Most PUBG gunfights happen between 20 and 100 metres, so spend most of your drill time at those distances. Long-range target practice looks impressive, but it trains a skill you use far less often.

3. Recoil control and sprays

Recoil control is pulling your mouse down and slightly to the side to counter the upward climb of your weapon. Take an M416 with a compensator and a vertical foregrip, stand 50 metres from a target, and spray a full magazine while pulling down smoothly. If the pattern climbs, pull down more; if it dips, pull down less. Practice until the pattern is tight, then repeat it while strafing. Learn the spray patterns of the M416 and the Beryl M762 and you will win far more medium-range fights.

4. Target switching

Target switching is flicking from one enemy to the next without hesitation. Set up two or three dummies at different angles and distances, knock one down with a short burst, then immediately flick to the next. In real matches, this is the skill that lets you win a one-versus-two fight, because the second enemy is often the one who kills you while you are still celebrating the first knock. Practice switching quickly and resist the urge to confirm a kill before moving on.

A 20-Minute Daily Routine

Here is a simple routine you can do before your first match of the day. It takes about twenty minutes and covers all four skills.

MinutesDrillWeapon
0-5Flick shots, one shot per targetKar98K / Mini 14
5-10Tracking moving targetsM416
10-15Spray and recoil controlM416 / Beryl M762
15-20Target switching between dummiesM416

Do this routine daily for two weeks and you will feel the difference in real matches: your first shot lands more often, your sprays stay on target longer, and you stop panicking when a second enemy appears. Keep a simple note of your progress, such as how many flick shots you hit out of twenty. Aim training works slowly, but it works permanently.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to improve my aim?

Most players notice a real difference after two to three weeks of daily 20-minute practice. Muscle memory builds gradually, so do not expect overnight results. The key is consistency: fifteen minutes every day beats two hours once a week.

Should I use an aim trainer app or PUBG training mode?

PUBG training mode is usually better because it uses the exact weapons, recoil patterns and sensitivity of the real game. External aim trainers can help with raw mouse control, but they cannot teach you the M416 spray pattern or how PUBG bullet drop feels. Use training mode first, and add an aim trainer only if you want extra flick practice.

What sensitivity should I use for aim training?

Use the same sensitivity you play matches with, so your practice transfers directly to real games. A common PC starting point is a general sensitivity of 40 to 45 with an ADS sensitivity of 35 to 40 for red dot sights. If your aim feels too fast and jumpy, lower it slightly; if you cannot turn fast enough, raise it. Then stop changing it.
