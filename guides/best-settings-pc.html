Best PUBG Settings for PC: FPS, Graphics and Visibility

PUBG: Battlegrounds Guide • Updated November 2026 • 7 min read

Your PUBG settings decide how much you can see, how smoothly the game runs and how quickly you can react. Two players with the same aim will perform very differently if one runs the game at 40 frames per second on high graphics and the other runs it at 144 frames per second on optimised settings. The good news is that you do not need an expensive computer to get a competitive setup. With the right mix of graphics, display and control settings, most mid-range PCs can run PUBG smoothly and look clear enough to spot enemies at distance. This guide walks through the best PC settings for FPS, visibility and comfort, with exact values you can copy.

Graphics Settings for Maximum FPS

PUBG is a demanding game, and high graphics settings cost frames without giving you a real advantage. Professional players almost all use low or medium settings, and you should too. Set your overall quality to Custom, then use these values. Anti-aliasing on Medium smooths jagged edges without costing much performance. Post-processing on Very Low removes fog and blur effects that hide enemies. Shadows on Very Low is the single biggest FPS boost, and it also makes enemies easier to spot because shadows no longer hide them. Textures on Medium keep the game looking decent while staying fast. Effects on Very Low and Foliage on Very Low are both important: low foliage makes grass thinner, so enemies hiding in fields are easier to see. View distance on Medium is enough for spotting enemies and vehicles. Turn off motion blur and V-Sync, and set sharpening to On. If your PC still struggles, drop textures to Low and lower your resolution scale to 90 percent.

Recommended graphics settings

  • Anti-aliasing: Medium
  • Post-processing: Very Low
  • Shadows: Very Low
  • Textures: Medium
  • Effects: Very Low
  • Foliage: Very Low
  • View distance: Medium
  • Motion blur: Off, V-Sync: Off, Sharpening: On

Display and Resolution Settings

Play in full screen mode for the best performance and the lowest input delay. Set your resolution to your monitor's native resolution, usually 1920 by 1080, and keep your refresh rate at the highest your monitor supports, such as 144Hz or 165Hz. If your PC cannot hold a stable frame rate at 1080p, drop to 1600 by 900 rather than lowering the resolution scale, because a clean lower resolution looks better than a blurry scaled one. Set your field of view to 90, which is the widest option and lets you see more of the battlefield without distorting the image too much. Keep brightness at 50 to 55 so dark interiors are readable without washing out the bright outdoor areas. If your monitor has a high refresh rate, make sure it is actually enabled in Windows display settings, because many players forget this step and play at 60Hz on a 144Hz screen.

Pro Tip: Turn on the in-game FPS counter in Settings, then test your settings in a training match. Aim for a stable frame rate that matches your monitor's refresh rate. A steady 100 FPS beats a spiky 144 FPS, so lower one setting at a time until the number stops jumping around.

Mouse and Keybind Settings

Your mouse sensitivity is personal, but a good starting point for most players is a general sensitivity of 40 to 50, a vertical sensitivity multiplier of 1.00, and an ADS sensitivity of around 35 to 45. Set your DPI to 400 or 800 on your mouse software, because low DPI with higher in-game sensitivity gives you smoother aim. Use the default keybinds for movement and weapons, but consider binding lean left and right to Q and E, which you already use, and crouch to C or Control, whichever feels natural. Bind your healing items to the number keys 7, 8, 9 and 0 so you can heal without opening the inventory. Set your map to M and your free look to Alt. Most importantly, keep your keybinds consistent: changing them every week stops your muscle memory from forming.

Audio and Gameplay Settings

Sound is half the game in PUBG. Set your master volume to 100 and use the HRTF option in the audio settings, which improves directional sound so you can tell where footsteps and gunshots come from. Use stereo headphones, not speakers, and turn off any virtual surround sound in your headphone software, because it interferes with the game's own sound engine. In gameplay settings, turn on the kill feed, set your crosshair colour to something bright like cyan or green, and enable the inventory screen character render only if your PC handles it well. Turn off the lobby music and the in-game background music so you can hear footsteps clearly. Finally, set your network settings to your closest server region to keep your ping low, and avoid playing on Wi-Fi if you can use a wired connection instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

What graphics settings do pro PUBG players use?

Most professional players use low or medium settings: shadows and foliage on Very Low, textures on Medium, anti-aliasing on Medium, and motion blur off. This maximises FPS and visibility instead of visual quality.

How many FPS do I need for competitive PUBG?

60 FPS is the minimum for smooth play, 100 FPS or more is ideal, and 144 FPS matches a 144Hz monitor perfectly. A stable frame rate matters more than a high peak number.

Should I use V-Sync in PUBG?

No. V-Sync adds input delay, which makes your aim feel sluggish. Turn it off and use your monitor's refresh rate settings instead.
