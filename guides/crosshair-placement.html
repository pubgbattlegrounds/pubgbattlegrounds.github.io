Crosshair Placement: The Skill That Wins Gunfights

PUBG: Battlegrounds Guide • Updated November 2026 • 7 min read

Most players think gunfights are won by flicking fast and aiming well in the moment. In reality, the fight is often decided before the enemy even appears, by where your crosshair was already pointing. Crosshair placement is the habit of keeping your aim at head height and pointed at the place an enemy is most likely to appear, so that when they do appear, you only need a tiny adjustment instead of a huge flick. A player with good crosshair placement shoots first and wins fights against players with faster reactions but worse positioning. It is the single most valuable aiming habit in PUBG, and it costs nothing to learn.

What Good Crosshair Placement Looks Like

Good crosshair placement has two rules. First, keep your crosshair at head height at all times. Headshots deal roughly double damage, and a Kar98K headshot can end a fight instantly. If your crosshair is already at head height when an enemy appears, your first shot is a headshot or a near miss. If it is pointing at the ground, you have to drag it up, and in that half-second the enemy shoots you first.

Second, keep your crosshair where the enemy will be, not where they are. When you move through a building, aim at the doorway you are about to enter. When you walk along a street, aim at the corner you are about to round. When you cross open ground, aim at the ridge or tree line where an enemy might be hiding. Your crosshair should always be tracking the most dangerous angle, the place an enemy could appear from at any moment. Players who aim at the sky or the ground while running are unprepared for every fight they walk into.

Pre-Aiming Corners and Doorways

Pre-aiming is the practical side of crosshair placement. Before you turn a corner, place your crosshair at the edge of the corner at head height, then swing around it smoothly so your aim is already on the space beyond. Before you enter a room, aim at the far corner or the spot behind the door where an enemy would hide. This feels slow at first, but a slow, pre-aimed corner clear wins far more fights than a fast, blind one.

Common angles to pre-aim

  • Doorways: aim at head height, slightly to the side where an enemy would stand behind the door.
  • Staircases: aim up the stairs at head height before you climb, since enemies often hold the top.
  • Ridges and hills: aim at the crest of the ridge, where a peeking enemy's head will appear.
  • Windows: aim at the window frame when passing a building, in case someone is watching from inside.
Pro Tip: When you are holding an angle, do not stare at the exact spot you expect the enemy. Aim slightly to the side of the corner, so that when they peek out, your crosshair is already on them instead of on the wall. This is called holding off-angle, and it makes your first shot land faster.

Crosshair Placement While Moving

Placement matters most while you are moving, because that is when most players let their aim wander. When you run across a field, your crosshair should be at head height, aimed at the horizon or the nearest cover, not at your feet. When you rotate through a town, sweep your crosshair from doorway to doorway. When you are looting, keep your crosshair toward the door, not toward the loot on the floor.

Your movement should support your placement. Strafe in short bursts rather than running in a straight line, and stop briefly to take accurate shots instead of firing wildly on the move. Crouching lowers your profile, but it also changes your head height relative to the enemy. The goal is to move in a way that keeps your crosshair ready at all times.

Adjusting for Distance

Crosshair placement changes with range. At close range, head height is the rule and your crosshair should be tight to corners. At medium range, around 50 to 100 metres, aim slightly above the enemy's head to account for bullet drop, and centre your crosshair where the enemy is likely to move. At long range, aim above the target and lead moving enemies by aiming slightly ahead of them. The principle stays the same: your crosshair should already be close to where the enemy will be.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I always aim at head height?

Almost always. Head height is the default because headshots deal the most damage and because an enemy's head is the first part of them to appear when they peek over a ridge or around a corner. The exception is when you know an enemy is prone or crouched behind low cover, in which case you should aim lower, at the part of them that is visible.

What is pre-aiming and why does it matter?

Pre-aiming means pointing your crosshair at a corner, doorway or angle before the enemy appears there, so you only need a small adjustment when they do. It matters because it removes the reaction-time gap: instead of seeing the enemy, then moving your aim, then shooting, you are already aiming at them and only need to fire.

Does crosshair placement matter on mobile too?

Yes, even more so. On mobile, flicking quickly is harder because of touch controls, so having your crosshair already in the right place saves you the hardest part of the shot. Good placement on mobile means fewer drags, faster first shots and far more won gunfights.
