PUBG Loot Guide: What to Pick Up and What to Leave

PUBG: Battlegrounds Guide • Updated November 2026 • 7 min read

Looting is the first skill every PUBG player learns and one of the last they truly master. The opening minute of a match decides whether you enter your first fight with an M416 and a level 3 vest or with a pistol and a bandage. Yet looting is not about grabbing everything you see: your backpack has limited space, the zone is always closing, and every second you spend staring at the floor is a second an enemy can use to line up a shot. Good looting is fast, selective and planned.

The Loot Priority Order

When you land, loot in this order: weapon, armour, healing, attachments, then utility. A gun comes first because an enemy can appear at any moment, and even a P92 pistol is better than bare hands. Next, grab a vest and helmet; a level 2 vest can be the difference between surviving a burst and going down instantly. Healing items come third, because a fight you survive with 20 health is still a fight you can win if you can heal. Attachments and throwables matter, but they are luxuries compared to the basics.

Think of your loadout as a kit with a purpose. Your primary weapon should handle medium-range fights, which is where most PUBG engagements happen. Your secondary weapon should cover the range your primary cannot: a sniper rifle or designated marksman rifle for long range, or a submachine gun or shotgun for close quarters. Two weapons using the same ammunition type will leave you starved for bullets, so mix your calibres when you can.

Weapons Worth Picking Up

Not every gun on the floor deserves a slot in your inventory. The M416 remains the most reliable all-round assault rifle thanks to its low recoil and five attachment slots, while the AKM hits harder at close range if you can control its kick. The Beryl M762 is a monster up close but punishes missed shots. For sniping, the Kar98K is the classic one-shot headshot rifle, and the Mini 14 is the most forgiving designated marksman rifle. Shotguns like the S12K dominate inside buildings, and the Vector shreds at point-blank range.

Weapons to leave behind

  • Pistols once you have a real primary weapon, unless you have nothing else.
  • The crossbow; its slow reload makes it a liability in real fights.
  • A second sniper rifle when you already carry one; take the ammo instead.
Pro Tip: Learn the quick-loot key (F on PC). Tapping F near an item picks it up instantly, and attachments you walk over will automatically fit your current weapons, saving precious seconds in a hot drop.

Armour, Helmets and Backpacks

Armour is not optional. A level 3 helmet protects you from a Kar98K headshot, and a level 3 vest absorbs far more damage than a level 1. Always upgrade when you see a higher level, but check the durability first: a level 3 vest at 10 percent durability protects worse than a fresh level 2 vest, and the same applies to helmets. A level 2 backpack carries 50 extra capacity and is usually enough, while a level 3 backpack is a luxury worth taking. A ghillie suit, found only in supply drops, is the best camouflage in the game, but it does not make you invisible; movement still gives you away.

Healing Items: What to Carry

Healing management separates experienced players from beginners. Bandages are slow and only heal to 75 health, so carry around five. First aid kits restore you to 75 health and are your main healing tool, so carry three to five. Med kits restore full health but are rare and slow, so one is enough. Energy drinks and painkillers fill your boost bar, which heals you above 75 health and speeds you up; carry five to ten combined.

Attachments and Throwables

Attachments turn a good gun into a great one. A compensator reduces recoil on the M416 and Beryl, a suppressor hides your muzzle flash and sound, and a vertical foregrip or half grip tightens your spray. Scopes matter too: a red dot for close range, a 3x or 4x scope for medium range, and a 6x or 8x scope for snipers. Extended magazines are almost always worth taking because a few extra bullets often decide a fight. Do not hoard attachments for weapons you do not own; drop them for your teammates or leave them behind.

Throwables are the most underrated loot in PUBG. Frags clear rooms, smokes create safe crossings and revive cover, and molotovs block doorways. Carry at least two frags and two smokes by the mid-game.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much ammunition should I carry?

For your primary weapon, around 200 to 250 rounds is plenty for most matches, plus 60 to 100 rounds for your secondary. Carrying 400 rounds of one calibre wastes backpack space that healing items and throwables need. In squads, share ammunition with teammates who are low instead of hoarding it.

Should I always take a level 3 vest over a level 2 vest?

Only if its durability is decent. A level 3 vest at very low durability protects less than a fresh level 2 vest, so check the condition before swapping. The same rule applies to helmets. A damaged level 3 helmet still stops a Kar98K headshot, though, so helmets are worth swapping more often.

What should I drop when my backpack is full?

Drop excess ammunition first, then attachments for weapons you do not own, then extra bandages beyond five. Never drop healing items, smokes or frags to make room for more bullets; fights are won by the player who can heal and reposition, not the one with the most spare ammunition.
