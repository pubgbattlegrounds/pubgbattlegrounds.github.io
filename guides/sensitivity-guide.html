PUBG Sensitivity Guide: Find Your Perfect Settings

PUBG: Battlegrounds Guide • Updated November 2026 • 7 min read

Sensitivity is the most personal setting in PUBG, and also the most misunderstood. It controls how far your aim moves when you move your mouse or your finger, and the wrong value makes even a good player miss easy shots. Too high and your crosshair flies past the target; too low and you cannot turn fast enough to track a moving enemy. There is no single perfect sensitivity that works for everyone, because it depends on your mouse DPI, your screen size, your grip and your playstyle. This guide explains how sensitivity works, gives you tested starting values for PC and mobile, and shows you a simple method to find your own perfect settings.

How Sensitivity Works

On PC, your effective sensitivity is your mouse DPI multiplied by your in-game sensitivity. A player with 400 DPI and 50 in-game sensitivity has the same turning speed as a player with 800 DPI and 25 in-game sensitivity. This is why copying a streamer's in-game number alone does not work: you need their DPI too. Most professional players use low DPI, usually 400 or 800, combined with a moderate in-game sensitivity, because low DPI gives smoother and more precise aim. On mobile, sensitivity is split into camera sensitivity, which controls how fast you look around, and ADS sensitivity, which controls how fast you aim while scoped. Lower ADS sensitivity for high-magnification scopes is normal, because a small movement at 8x covers a huge distance on screen. The key idea is the same on both platforms: your sensitivity should let you turn 180 degrees comfortably and track a moving target without overshooting.

The 180-degree test

  • Stand in the training ground and mark a point behind you.
  • Swipe your mouse or finger across your mousepad or screen in one comfortable motion.
  • You should be able to turn 180 degrees in that single motion without lifting or running out of space.
  • If you turn too far, lower your sensitivity. If you cannot reach 180 degrees, raise it.

Recommended Starting Values for PC

Use these values as a starting point and adjust from there. Set your mouse DPI to 800, your general sensitivity to 40, your targeting sensitivity to 40, and your scoping sensitivity to 40. For ADS, use 45 for red dot and holographic sights, 40 for 2x scopes, 35 for 3x scopes, 30 for 4x scopes, 25 for 6x scopes, and 20 for 8x scopes. Set your vertical sensitivity multiplier to 1.00 so vertical and horizontal aim feel the same. These numbers give you enough speed to react to close-range fights while staying precise enough for mid-range sprays. If you play on a large mousepad, you can go slightly lower; if your space is small, go slightly higher. Avoid the common mistake of using a very high sensitivity because it feels fast: fast is useless if you cannot stop on the target.

Pro Tip: Your ADS sensitivity should decrease as your scope magnification increases. A good rule of thumb is to drop it by about 5 points for each step up in magnification, so your 8x scope feels steady and your red dot still feels quick.

Recommended Starting Values for Mobile

On mobile, start with camera sensitivity of 100 percent for third person and 110 percent for first person. For ADS, use 65 percent for red dot and holographic, 55 percent for 2x, 40 percent for 3x, 30 percent for 4x, 20 percent for 6x, and 15 percent for 8x. If you use the gyroscope, start at 250 percent for third person, 200 percent for red dot, and 120 percent for 4x scopes, then tune in steps of 10. Players with small phones can use slightly higher values, while tablet players should go lower. The same rule applies as on PC: adjust one slider at a time, play three or four matches, and only then decide whether the change helped.

How to Fine-Tune Your Sensitivity

Finding your perfect sensitivity takes about a week, and the method is simple. First, set the starting values from this guide. Second, play in training mode and shoot the moving targets at different ranges. If your crosshair keeps passing the target and you have to correct backwards, your sensitivity is too high. If you lag behind the target and cannot catch up, it is too low. Third, take the settings into real matches and watch your close-range fights: these reveal sensitivity problems fastest, because you need to turn and track quickly. Fourth, change your sensitivity by only 5 points at a time, never by 20 or 30. Big jumps reset your muscle memory and make everything feel wrong. Finally, once you find a value that works, stop changing it. The best players in the world keep the same sensitivity for months or years, and their consistency comes from that stability, not from constantly tweaking.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best sensitivity in PUBG?

There is no single best sensitivity. A good starting point on PC is 800 DPI with 40 in-game sensitivity, and on mobile it is 100 percent camera sensitivity with 65 percent red dot ADS. Tune from there using the 180-degree test.

Should I copy a pro player's sensitivity?

Use it as a starting point only. Pro settings are tuned to their mouse, DPI, screen and grip, so their exact numbers may feel wrong for you. Adjust in small steps until the settings fit your hands.

How often should I change my sensitivity?

As rarely as possible. Change one value by 5 points, test it for several matches, and then leave it alone. Constant changes prevent your muscle memory from developing and hurt your aim in the long run.
