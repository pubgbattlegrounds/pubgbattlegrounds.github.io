Solo vs Squad Strategy: Play Smart in Every Mode

PUBG: Battlegrounds Guide • Updated November 2026 • 7 min read

PUBG plays like a different game depending on how many teammates you have beside you. In solo mode, every decision is yours alone: there is no revive, no backup and no one to watch your back. In squads, you have numbers on your side, but only if you actually play together. Each mode rewards a different mindset, and this guide explains how to think, move and fight in each one.

The Core Difference: Information vs Firepower

In solo, your biggest weakness is information. You can only look in one direction at a time, so enemies can always approach from an angle you are not watching. Your biggest strength is stealth: a single player makes almost no noise and can hide in a bush or a dip in the terrain without being spotted. In squads, the situation reverses. Four pairs of eyes cover far more ground, so ambushes are rarer, but four players are loud and visible, and a single well-placed grenade can end a whole push.

That trade-off should shape everything you do. Solo players should play quiet, patient and opportunistic, taking fights only when the odds favour them. Squad players should play confident and coordinated, using their numbers to take space and overwhelm isolated enemies. Duos sit in the middle: you have one partner to revive you, but you still cannot afford careless rotations.

Solo Strategy: Patience Wins

In solo mode, survival is a skill in itself. You do not need to win every fight; you need to avoid the fights you cannot win. Land somewhere with decent loot but light traffic, such as the smaller compounds on Erangel like Lipovka, rather than dropping straight into the centre of a hot drop. Loot quickly, grab a scoped rifle and an assault rifle, and move with the zone rather than chasing kills.

Always keep hard cover within a few steps: a rock, a tree, a ridge or a building. Open fields are death traps for a solo player because nobody can trade kills for you if you get knocked. When the circle shrinks, rotate early and take the edge of the zone so you only have to watch one direction. If you hear a fight nearby, let it play out and third-party the winner while they are healing.

When to fight in solo

  • Fight when you have the drop on an enemy who has not seen you.
  • Fight when you hold high ground or hard cover and they are in the open.
  • Do not fight when you are out in the open, low on health or unsure where the enemy is.
  • Do not chase a knock across the map; a second enemy is often waiting.
Pro Tip: In solo, carry at least two smoke grenades at all times. A smoke on yourself buys you the seconds you need to heal or reposition, and there is no teammate to throw one for you.

Squad Strategy: Play as One Unit

Squads are won by teamwork, not by four individuals who happen to share a lobby. Before the match, agree on a landing spot and a basic plan: who loots which building and when you rotate. During the game, stay within 50 to 100 metres of each other. If one player wanders off to a compound 300 metres away, they will die alone and the team will be a man down for the rest of the match.

Communication is the real weapon in squads. Call out enemy positions with a compass direction and a landmark: "two enemies, 240 degrees, behind the blue house" is useful; "over there" is not. When a fight starts, spread out instead of bunching up behind the same rock, so one grenade cannot wipe the team. When a teammate is knocked, smoke them immediately, keep one player covering the revive, and have the rest hold angles on the enemy. Never let the whole team stop to revive while enemies are pushing.

Duo Strategy: The Middle Ground

Duos reward a mix of solo patience and squad aggression. You have a partner to revive you, so you can take slightly riskier fights than in solo, but one mistake can still end the match. Stay 20 to 40 metres apart so you cover different angles and cannot both be caught by one grenade. Share loot fairly, especially scopes, healing items and ammunition, and in the final circles decide in advance who takes the first shot so you fire together.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is solo harder than squad mode in PUBG?

It depends on your strengths. Solo is harder in that one mistake ends the match and you have no revive, but it is easier in that you control every decision and nobody gives away your position. Many players find squads harder to climb in because success depends on teammates who may not communicate or stick together.

Should I play aggressively or passively in solo?

Play selectively aggressive. Take fights where you have the advantage, such as an unaware enemy or a strong position, and avoid fair fights in the open. The best solo players are not the ones who hide all game, but the ones who choose their moments and never fight on the enemy's terms.

What is the most common squad mistake?

Splitting up. Squads that land in different towns, rotate separately or push fights one at a time lose constantly. Stay close, communicate clearly and fight together, and you will beat teams with better aim but worse coordination.
